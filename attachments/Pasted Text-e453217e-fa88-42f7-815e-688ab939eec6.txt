WNRG LN vs IOGP LN — Full Comparison

The One-Line Answer

IOGP is the purer energy play. WNRG is the more resilient farm line. They are not interchangeable — they are structurally different portfolios that happen to share the same sector label.

WNRG is a full energy value chain ETF. Half its weight sits in integrated majors — companies like ExxonMobil, Shell, TotalEnergies, and BP — which have downstream refining, chemicals, and increasingly LNG and power businesses that partially insulate them from the raw oil price. Integrated companies are more shielded from volatility due to global and downstream value chain diversification. Bloomberg Intelligence

IOGP is a pure upstream extraction bet. At 98.36% E&P, it owns companies whose entire business is finding and producing oil and gas. When the oil price moves, IOGP moves with it — amplified by operating leverage. Higher beta E&P and oil-services ETFs show more responsiveness in recent movements compared to producers, refiners, and integrated companies. Bloomberg Intelligence

The Regime Analysis: When Each Wins

IOGP wins when:

Oil price surges sharply — pure E&P operating leverage amplifies the move. Oil E&Ps are discounting approximately $65 WTI at current prices, meaning any sustained move above that level flows directly to free cash flow. Barclays

Deep stagflation — the 1973–1980 analogue. Pure upstream E&P was the highest-returning sub-sector in that decade. Upstream sector valuations remain compelling at approximately 5x 2027E EV/DACF for oil-weighted E&Ps. Barclays

Geopolitical supply shock — E&P companies with production assets benefit directly from price spikes. Energy equities have retraced wartime gains, with 2027 unlevered free cash flow yields remaining compelling at approximately 9.5% for diversified/oil-weighted E&Ps. Barclays

WNRG wins when:

Oil price is volatile but not directional — integrated majors' downstream businesses (refining, chemicals, LNG) provide earnings stability when the upstream is choppy. Integrated companies are traditionally more defensive due to their asset composition. Bloomberg Intelligence

Dividend income matters — Shell's dividend breakeven is approximately $28/bbl, with a consistent $3bn quarterly buyback program. UBS Integrated majors distribute more reliably than pure E&Ps.

Energy transition is a concern — European integrated majors' upstream portfolios are increasingly skewed toward natural gas, with expectations to reach at least 50% by 2030, driven by long-term demand growth and gas' role in the energy mix. Bloomberg Intelligence

Regime uncertainty — the diversified value chain (refining, midstream, services) means WNRG participates in energy sector strength without being fully dependent on the oil price direction.

The Farm Verdict

The Principia framework asks one question: which line earns its allocation across the most economic weathers without losing the farm in the worst one?

For the farm as currently constructed, WNRG LN is the correct first addition. Here is why:

The farm already has ICOM (broad commodities) providing indirect oil price exposure. Adding IOGP on top would create a double-concentration in raw commodity beta — two lines that both fall hard when oil reverses.

WNRG's integrated major exposure (50.32%) adds something the farm genuinely lacks: large-cap energy equity with dividend income, balance sheet strength, and downstream diversification. Shell, XOM, and TotalEnergies are not the same risk as ICOM.

WNRG's OCF of 0.30% vs IOGP's 0.55% is a 25bp annual drag difference — meaningful over a decade of contributions.

WNRG's larger AUM ($609M vs $449M) means tighter spreads and better liquidity for a GBP ISA where the position will be built slowly via monthly contributions.

IOGP belongs on the watch list, not the buy list — yet. If the regime shifts decisively into deep stagflation (Brent sustains above $120, CPI re-accelerates above 7%), IOGP's pure E&P beta becomes the highest-conviction line in the entire universe screen. At that point, the farm could hold both: WNRG as the structural energy line and IOGP as a tactical overweight — but that would require a seventeenth line, which the protocol does not permit. The signed sentence stays the hand.

The one scenario where IOGP is the better first addition: if the farm's goal is maximum stagflation protection and the engine (VWRP) is already providing sufficient integrated-major exposure via its ~4% energy sector weight. In that case, IOGP's pure upstream beta adds something genuinely incremental. But at 45% VWRP, the engine's energy weight is only ~1.8% of the total farm — not enough to make IOGP redundant. WNRG remains the priority.

Suggested Principia line name for WNRG: "The Derrick" — the structure that sits above the well, not the well itself.
