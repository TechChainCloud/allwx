Why VAGS Still Deserves Its Weight — The Counter-Case

Before modelling the trim, the counter-case must be stated plainly. VAGS is not broken. It is doing exactly what it was designed to do: nothing, in a Reflation regime. Its job is to be loud in a deflationary slowdown — G↓, I↓, policy rates falling — and that regime is not today's regime. The −0.945 Sharpe is the cost of holding deflation insurance in an inflationary world. The question is whether 17% is the right amount of that insurance, or whether 14% is sufficient.

The Reason the House Does Not Do This

Three reasons, in order of importance.

First, the regime can rotate faster than a contribution cycle. VAGS at 14% vs 17% is a 3pp difference in deflation coverage. If G rolls below 50 PMI — the walk-back trigger scenario — VAGS is the line that saves the book. Trimming it now, at the exact moment the stagflation overlay is strengthening, is selling insurance after the storm has started.

Second, the mechanism for this change is contribution-routing, not a sell order. The house does not trim by selling. It trims by routing new contributions away from VAGS until drift corrects. If VAGS is currently above 17% in the live portfolio, the next contribution naturally routes elsewhere. If it is at 17%, there is no drift breach — no action is warranted under the rule hierarchy.

Third, VWRP at 26.8% is 0.2pp from its 27% cap. Routing 3pp to VWRP gets the portfolio one bad month away from a hard-cap breach. That is not a comfortable position to lock in deliberately.

The Honest Conclusion

VAGS at 17% has the worst risk-adjusted case of any line in the pie for the current regime. The trim to 14% improves the portfolio on every quantitative metric that matters today. The house does not make that move because the house is not optimised for today — it is optimised for every day, including the one where G drops to 48 PMI and VAGS is suddenly the only line earning. The 3pp between 17% and 14% is the price of that optionality. Whether it is worth paying is the one question the rules do not answer for you.

What the rules do say: if VAGS drifts below 14% on its own — through market moves, not through a sell order — the next contribution routes back to it. That is the floor doing its job. The house does not pre-empt the drift; it responds to it.
