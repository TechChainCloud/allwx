What the Data Is Actually Saying

The honest answer to the question

There is one instrument in the candidate universe that improves both return and risk-adjusted return simultaneously, passes the regime role gate, and is available as a UCITS London-listed ETF: IWVL (iShares MSCI World Value Factor).

Over 5 years, IWVL returned 17.19% annualised vs VWRP's 11.09% — a 610bp annual advantage.

Its volatility is 16.29% vs VWRP's 12.99% — only 330bp more vol for 610bp more return.

Its Sharpe of 0.84 is the highest of any equity candidate and second only to SGLN (0.94) in the entire universe tested.

Replacing 10% of VWRP with IWVL (Variant A) raises expected portfolio return from 7.81% to 8.42% — a 61bp improvement — with only a 33bp increase in portfolio volatility.

Why IWVL works in this framework

Value factor stocks are structurally cheaper on earnings and book value multiples. In a Reflation regime — the current regime — value outperforms growth because rising rates compress the long-duration growth stock multiples that dominate VWRP's cap-weighted structure. IWVL's +34.7% YTD vs VWRP's +13.0% YTD is not a coincidence. It is the value factor doing exactly what it is supposed to do when real yields are rising and the discount rate is elevated. The regime alignment is live, not theoretical.

What does not work — and why the data eliminates it

INXG (UK linkers): eliminated. 5Y return −39.8%, max drawdown −50.87%, Sharpe −0.66. The inflation shock that ITPS was designed to absorb destroyed UK linkers because of their extreme duration (effective duration ~20 years) combined with the UK's specific fiscal/gilt crisis in 2022. This is the single most important finding in the data tonight. The instrument that intuitively sounds like "the right inflation hedge for a GBP investor" was the worst performer in the entire candidate set. Do not add INXG at current real yield levels.

MVOL: eliminates itself. Lower vol than VWRP (10.71% vs 12.99%) but also lower return (5.27% vs 11.09%). It reduces risk but reduces return by more. The Sharpe of 0.17 is worse than simply holding CSH2 at 3.5%. It fails the regime role gate — it is VWRP with the return extracted.

IWMO (momentum): fails the vol gate. Similar 5Y return to VWRP (11.35% vs 11.09%) but with 19.26% volatility — the highest of any candidate. More vol for no more return. Momentum is a factor that works in trending markets and crashes in reversals. It adds no regime coverage the machine does not already have.

SEML: low Sharpe, high diversification value. The Sharpe of −0.20 looks damning. But SEML's max drawdown of −11.11% is the lowest of any candidate — lower than VWRP, lower than VAGS, lower than ITPS. It adds genuine EM carry and geographic diversification of the bond sleeve at the lowest drawdown cost of any instrument tested. Its value is not in the Sharpe — it is in the correlation it does not have with the rest of the book. This is a Phase 2 addition at £10,000, not a current action.

3. The rebalancing discipline itself — available now, at any book size

This is not a new instrument. It is the existing machine operated correctly.
