The Honest Assessment

A pure Reflation optimizer would look like this: overweight VWRP and ICOM, zero VAGS and IDTL, trim SGLN, max SMGB and DFNS. That portfolio would have outperformed the house significantly over the past 12 months — and it would be destroyed in a regime rotation to Goldilocks or deflation.

The house is not a Reflation optimizer. It is an all-weather engine with a Reflation tilt already baked in — VWRP at 23.8% is the largest single line, ICOM at 8.5% is the goods-inflation hedge, and the satellite sleeve is weighted toward Reflation beneficiaries (SMGB, DFNS, COPA, XDWI). The drag lines — VAGS at 17%, IDTL at 5.1% — are the insurance premium against being wrong about the regime.

The three genuine tensions in the current regime are:

VAGS at 17% is the biggest drag. It is the correct size for an all-weather book. It is the wrong size for a Reflation trade. The house accepts this explicitly — conditioning VAGS on this week's GT10 means you do not have a house, you have a momentum strategy.

ICOM at 8.5% is arguably undersized for a regime where it wins in both primary and overlay simultaneously. The house does not chase — the next contribution on 3 October is the correct mechanism to address drift, not a discretionary top-up.

SGLN at 10.2% is being taxed by the short-term real-yield channel while the long-term debt-cycle thesis remains intact. A regime-optimizer would trim. The house holds — and that is the right call.

Conclusion: The house is optimised for durability across all four regime boxes, with a structural tilt toward Reflation. It is not optimised for the current regime in isolation — and that is the point. The under-earning is the feature, not the bug.
