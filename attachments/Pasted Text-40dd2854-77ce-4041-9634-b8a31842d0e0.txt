Chapter 1 — What Invictus Prime Is

Invictus Prime is a 15-line, 2-sleeve, all-weather ISA portfolio built on Ray Dalio's Economic Machine framework. It is held in a T212 Stocks & Shares ISA with AutoInvest set to execute on the 3rd of every month at £200/month. It was locked permanently on 7 September 2026 at 23:08 BST, replacing all prior versions.

The design philosophy is singular: every line has a defined job in each of the four economic regime boxes. The portfolio is not a forecast. It does not bet on one outcome. It is a structure built to survive and compound through all four regimes — Reflation, Goldilocks, Stagflation, and Deflation — without requiring active management decisions between contribution dates.

Chapter 2 — How the Portfolio Was Built and Why

Invictus Prime was designed from first principles using Dalio's Economic Machine. The framework identifies four regime states defined by the direction of growth and inflation. Every asset class has a defined behaviour in each box. The portfolio's job is to hold lines that collectively earn a positive real return in all four simultaneously — not by predicting which regime comes next, but by owning the right instruments for each.

The key design decisions made during the build:

VAGS trimmed from 17% to 13.5% — worst Sharpe of any line (−0.945). Headwind in both Reflation and Stagflation. Trimmed to floor.

ICOM increased from 8.5% to 9.6% — the only line that earns in both Reflation and Stagflation. Was undersized relative to its regime utility.

INXG added at 3.8% — new line. UK CPI contract. Correlation with ITPS: 0.019. Genuinely uncorrelated to the USD inflation leg. Covers GBP-denominated inflation risk that ITPS cannot.

IGLS added at 2.8% — new line. UK short gilt. Duration 2.5yr. Ladders the bond sleeve and earns GBP carry without long-end term premium risk.

XDWH trimmed from 2.85% to 1.8% — lowest regime fit in the satellite sleeve. Funds the additions above.

The portfolio was compared against a 20-instrument all-weather design during construction. Gold/equity correlation confirmed at 0.009. Silver/bond correlation at 0.019. Oil confirmed negative correlation with both stocks and bonds. The correlation matrix is the engine — diversification does the work that return alone cannot.

Chapter 5 — What Invictus Prime Cannot Do

This was addressed directly and honestly. Invictus Prime cannot reliably deliver 10–15% annual average returns over 30 years. The S&P 500 has averaged ~10.5% nominal over 100 years — with crashes of −50%, −57%, and −34% along the way. Prime was explicitly designed to not be that portfolio.

The three paths to 10–15% CAGR and their real costs:

100% VWRP: ~10–12% CAGR. Worst year ~−40%. Abandons the all-weather framework entirely.

60–70% equity tilt: ~9.5–10.5% CAGR. Worst year ~−20%. Partial all-weather. Requires rewriting the lock file.

Leveraged risk parity (Bridgewater's actual method): ~12–15% CAGR. Not available in a retail ISA. Structurally closed.

The honest lever that costs nothing in risk: contributions. Doubling contributions from £200 to £400/month at 8.2% CAGR is mathematically equivalent to adding ~2.5pp of annual return. The contribution schedule does the heavy lifting the portfolio cannot.

Chapter 8 — Key Concepts Defined

TER (Total Expense Ratio)

The annual percentage of fund assets deducted automatically to cover operating costs. It is silent, continuous, and compounds against you every year regardless of market conditions. It is the only guaranteed negative return in the portfolio. Prime's weighted TER of ~0.14% is institutional-grade for a retail ISA. Every 0.10pp of TER saved over 30 years on a growing portfolio is worth approximately £8,000–£12,000 in terminal value.

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

The single rate that, applied consistently every year, takes a starting value to an ending value over a given period. It is the geometric mean — not the arithmetic mean. A portfolio that gains +50% then loses −33% has a CAGR of +0.25%, not +8.5%. Volatility destroys CAGR: large drawdowns require proportionally larger recoveries. A −50% loss requires a +100% gain just to break even. This is why Prime's worst-year estimate of −12.6% matters more than it looks — it keeps the recovery tax manageable.

Sharpe Ratio

(Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate) ÷ Portfolio Volatility. Measures excess return per unit of risk. Prime's modelled Sharpe of 0.44 beats 60/40 (0.37), 100% equity (0.38), and Sunny-Day (~0.35). The reshaped Prime with DBMF targets 0.48–0.52.

Value at Risk (VaR)

The maximum loss over a defined period at a given confidence level. At 7.5% annual volatility, Prime's estimated 95% 1-month VaR is approximately −3.0% (£300 on £10,000). Not yet formally computed using historical simulation against Prime's actual correlation matrix — this is on the pending analysis list.

Monte Carlo Simulation

Running thousands of randomised simulations of possible future return sequences to produce a probability distribution of terminal outcomes. The output is a fan chart showing the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th percentile terminal values. The full 30-year Monte Carlo was run today for both the original and reshaped Prime specifications.

Chapter 10 — 23x5 US Equities: What It Means

The SEC has approved a 23-hours-per-day, 5-days-per-week equity trading framework. Cboe EDGX, NYSE Arca, NASDAQ, 24X, and Members Exchange are the participating venues. NSCC has received approval for 24x5 clearing. NYSE American has extended options trading hours as of 2 September 2026.

The current reality: overnight trading accounts for only 0.52% of US equity volume in 2026. The direction is clear; the scale is not yet there. Interactive Brokers reported overnight volumes nearly tripling year-over-year in Q1 2026 to 8 million trades.

For Invictus Prime, the implications are three:

Higher overnight volatility on macro events — VWRP and SMGB will gap more sharply on CPI prints, FOMC decisions, and geopolitical events. The portfolio does not change. The noise around it does. The rule is unchanged: do not check the portfolio between contribution dates.

Better long-term price discovery — as overnight sessions mature and liquidity deepens, ETF NAV tracking improves globally. Positive for long-term holders over a 3–5 year horizon.

Stronger case for DBMF — managed futures profits from trends and dislocations. A world with 23x5 equity trading creates more overnight gaps and momentum events for trend-following strategies to exploit. The structural case for DBMF in the reshaped Prime is strengthened by this development.
